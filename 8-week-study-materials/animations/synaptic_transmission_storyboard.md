# Synaptic Transmission Animation Storyboard

**Audience:** High-school student, first exposure, no calculus.

**Placement:** Chapter 3, Mini Lab or Simulation.

**Learning goal:** Explain how a chemical synapse turns a presynaptic spike into a postsynaptic graded voltage change that may be excitatory or inhibitory.

**Misconception this prevents:** A synapse does not automatically cause a postsynaptic spike; most synaptic events create small graded changes that must combine with other inputs before threshold is reached.

## Frame 1. Presynaptic Spike Arrives

- **Onscreen narration:** A spike reaches the presynaptic terminal at the end of an axon.
- **Visual instruction:** Show an axon terminal with a voltage pulse arriving from the left and vesicles waiting near the presynaptic membrane.

## Frame 2. Calcium Channels Open

- **Onscreen narration:** The arriving spike opens voltage-gated Ca2+ channels in the terminal.
- **Visual instruction:** Highlight Ca2+ channels in the presynaptic membrane and animate Ca2+ ions entering from outside the terminal.

## Frame 3. Vesicles Move To The Membrane

- **Onscreen narration:** Ca2+ helps synaptic vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane.
- **Visual instruction:** Move several neurotransmitter-filled vesicles toward the membrane and show one vesicle merging with it.

## Frame 4. Neurotransmitter Crosses The Cleft

- **Onscreen narration:** Neurotransmitter molecules diffuse across the synaptic cleft.
- **Visual instruction:** Release small neurotransmitter dots into the gap between cells and move them toward receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.

## Frame 5. Receptors Open Ion Channels

- **Onscreen narration:** Neurotransmitter binding changes postsynaptic ion channels.
- **Visual instruction:** Show neurotransmitter binding to receptors and open nearby channels in the postsynaptic membrane.

## Frame 6. EPSP Example

- **Onscreen narration:** If the postsynaptic voltage moves toward threshold, the graded signal is an EPSP.
- **Visual instruction:** Animate positive charge entering the postsynaptic cell and draw a small upward voltage bump labeled EPSP.

## Frame 7. IPSP Example

- **Onscreen narration:** If the postsynaptic voltage moves away from threshold or becomes harder to spike, the graded signal is an IPSP.
- **Visual instruction:** Show an alternate receptor opening pathway with a small downward or stabilizing voltage change labeled IPSP.

## Frame 8. Inputs Must Sum

- **Onscreen narration:** The neuron fires only if combined inputs bring the trigger zone to threshold.
- **Visual instruction:** Zoom out to show several synapses on dendrites and soma, draw small EPSP and IPSP bumps adding into one combined trace, and mark a threshold line.

## Student Check

- Pause on the final frame and ask: Which part is chemical communication, which part is a voltage change, and why does one synapse not always cause a spike?
