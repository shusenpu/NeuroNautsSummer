# Action Potential Animation Storyboard

**Audience:** High-school student, first exposure, no calculus.

**Placement:** Chapter 4, Mini Lab or Simulation.

**Learning goal:** Explain an action potential as a timed sequence of membrane voltage changes caused by voltage-gated Na+ and K+ channel behavior.

**Misconception this prevents:** A stronger stimulus does not make one action potential much taller; once threshold is crossed, the spike follows a stereotyped sequence, and stronger input is usually represented by spike timing or spike number.

## Frame 1. Resting Membrane

- **Onscreen narration:** At rest, the inside of the neuron is negative compared with the outside.
- **Visual instruction:** Show an axon membrane cross-section with V_m labeled near -70 mV, many K+ ions inside, many Na+ ions outside, and voltage-gated Na+ and K+ channels closed.

## Frame 2. Graded Input Moves Toward Threshold

- **Onscreen narration:** Small inputs can depolarize the trigger zone toward threshold.
- **Visual instruction:** Add a small upward voltage trace from -70 mV toward -55 mV and show a few positive charges entering near the axon initial segment.

## Frame 3. Threshold Is Crossed

- **Onscreen narration:** When threshold is crossed, voltage-gated Na+ channels begin opening quickly.
- **Visual instruction:** Mark the threshold line at about -55 mV and highlight several Na+ channels switching from closed to open.

## Frame 4. Rising Phase

- **Onscreen narration:** Na+ entry makes the inside less negative, which opens even more Na+ channels.
- **Visual instruction:** Animate Na+ ions moving inward through open channels while the voltage trace rises steeply toward positive values.

## Frame 5. Peak And Na+ Inactivation

- **Onscreen narration:** The spike stops rising when many Na+ channels inactivate.
- **Visual instruction:** Show the voltage trace near its peak, change open Na+ channels into an inactivated blocked state, and begin opening K+ channels.

## Frame 6. Repolarization

- **Onscreen narration:** K+ movement helps bring the membrane voltage back down.
- **Visual instruction:** Animate K+ ions moving outward through open K+ channels while the voltage trace falls toward negative values.

## Frame 7. After-Hyperpolarization

- **Onscreen narration:** Some K+ channels stay open briefly, so the membrane can become more negative than rest.
- **Visual instruction:** Dip the voltage trace below the resting line and show a few K+ channels still open after the main spike.

## Frame 8. Refractory Recovery

- **Onscreen narration:** The channels reset, and the membrane returns toward its resting state.
- **Visual instruction:** Return V_m toward -70 mV, close most K+ channels, reset Na+ channels to closed-ready, and label this interval refractory recovery.

## Student Check

- Pause on the final frame and ask: Which phase is caused mostly by Na+ entry, which phase is helped by K+ movement, and why does the spike not keep rising forever?
